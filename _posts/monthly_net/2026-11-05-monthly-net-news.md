## This Month In Amateur Radio History

- November 1919 - Secretary of the Navy Josephus Daniels tries to get the Navy a total monopoly on all wireless communications. The ARRL's "blue card" appeal saves the concept of private radio operations. Amateurs get back on the air in November, 1919.
- November 15, 1945 - FCC held hearings on post-war VHF allocations, in which there were 231 witnesses and 4200 pages of testimony. A major battle develops over postwar frequency allocations. The Hams, FM Broadcasting and Television groups, all fight over the low end of the VHF spectrum between 44-108 MHz (Mc). On November 15, 1945, amateurs are allowed back on the air--but just on 10 & 2 meters only.
- November 2nd, 1920 – With only about 100 people were listening, Frank Conrad (8XK), flipped the switch for the first time and the first broadcast from a licensed radio station occurred at 8 p.m. on Nov. 2, 1920. It was Pittsburgh’s KDKA, and the station was broadcasting the results of that year’s presidential election (Warren G. Harding won… if you aren’t following early 20 century politics). At the time, I doubt Mr. Conrad could have envisioned just how profoundly broadcast media would transform political life.
- November 22nd, 1967 - The FCC instituted "incentive licensing," (or “Incentive punishment” as it was sometimes called) a tiered approach for licensing that had multiple levels to ostensibly encourage amateurs to upgrade. In reality most amateurs lost up to 50% of the frequencies they usually operated (General, Technician and Novice), while giving exclusive use of some whole bands to the Amateur and Extra licenses. Incentive licensing (or incentive punishment as some have called it) was ended on November 22, 1969, though some of the effects are still in the band plans.
- November 1, 1969, the ARRL Board of Directors met and came to a decision on the fate of Technician license. Originally, Technicians were considered “experimenters” which required a more difficult test, and needed to additionally have another amateur radio license (usually Novice). In an editorial in the December 1969 issue of "QST" entitled "Technicians as Communicators", the ARRL's new position was stated--Technicians were no longer just experimenters, but rather full fledged communicators.
- Cause I am a little late: October 4, 1957 - The Soviets launched Sputnik. Thousands of hams tuned in the weak beacon from the satellite on 20.005 and 40.002 MHz. Amateur Radio received a lot of publicity, as across the nation, many local papers ran articles on the hometown hams and the "signals from space". Notably, radio enthusiasts at APL noticed the Doppler effect of the radio broadcasts, and it created the idea for GPS signals that we use today (through the Transit satellite program)
- November 3, 1957 - The Soviets launched Sputnik 2, with the first living being onboard, a mix bread dog named “Laika”.

## W3APL Last Month’s Events

The club received a donation of some great reading material. A heartfelt THANK YOU for your generosity to the club.

Members from the W3APL club, supported the Scouting America (formerly Boy Scouts of America) Jamboree on the Air/Jamboree on the Internet (JOTA/JOTI) events. JOTA/JOTI is the worlds largest Scouting event connecting scouts from around the world together through Amateur Radio. Scouts were able to learn about Morse Code, conduct Amateur Radio Direction Finding, practice the phonetic alphabet, as well as get online and over the air to talk to scouts in Hong Kong, Saudi Arabia, England, Chile, Brazil and around the US. Maybe we will have some new radio operators.

A club member attended the 17 annual SOTA Appalachia Workshop down in Virginia, where there were discussions on SOTA tactics and technique, followed by going out and activating local summits.

Club members were able to engineer the rotators and VHF/UHF satellite antenna to operate. Not only were they able to fix the electronics and electrical connections, but were also able to design a cool interface that shows where the antennas are pointed, control the rotation, and even track specific satellites. Thank you Both!
