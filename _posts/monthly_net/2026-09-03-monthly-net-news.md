## This Month In Amateur Radio History

- September is [National Preparedness Month][ares].
- September 1915 – The first mention of amateur radio appeared in a [local newspaper, the Asheville Citizen][article], which quoted President Taft about domestic security.
- September 1917 – Due to the Great War (World War 1), the US Government banned amateur radio activity. With no radio activity permitted and 80% of the amateurs at war, ARRL ceased publication of QST magazine.
- September 2, 1938 - The new Maxim Memorial Station, W1AW, was dedicated at [225 Main Street in Newington, CT][w1aw]. The Station was in memory of [Hiram Percy Maxim][maxim], the Founder and first President of the [ARRL][arrl], who died in February 1936.
- September 13, 1938, [Ross Hull][hull], Editor of QST, died after being electrocuted in his home. He had been working on a homebrew TV receiver. So video did actually kill the radio star…
- September 11, 1958 - the FCC came to a decision: "our" 11 meter band would be removed from us and turned over to the new [Class C and Class D Citizens Band][cb]
- September 1965, Hurricane Betsy hit Florida, the Bahamas, and the central US Gulf Cost. Gerald E. “Jerry” Murphy, K8YUW, then 28 years old, was stationed at the U.S. Naval Mobile Construction Battalion Center in Davisville, Rhode Island. “During his time off, he handled countless phone patches and messages to and from military-deployed personnel as a member of the [Intercontinental Amateur Radio Net (IARN)][iarn] on 14.320 MHz. The public was becoming concerned. In 1965, there wasn’t any access to 24-hour news and weather reports. Murphy suggested to the IARN net manager to move those interested in the storm up 5 kHz to get them off the net, and the net manager agreed. Marcy Rice, KZ5MM, located in the Canal Zone (Panama), followed Murphy, and together they established the first Hurricane Watch Net on 14.325 MHz.”
- September 11th, 2001 – Due to the Terrorist attacks, Ham radio operators mobilized to New York, and Pennsylvania to support disaster relief efforts.
- September 2005 and 2017 – Amateur radio operators deployed to Louisiana (Hurricane Katrina) and Puerto Rico (Hurricane Maria) to support the American Red Cross to help restore communications networks.

## Radio News

### Huge Grant For Next Generation Problem Solvers

[ARRL The National Association for Amateur Radio®][arrl] [announced today a historic and transformational $1 million][gift] gift from Dr. James M. Galm, W8WTS. An entrepreneur and amateur radio operator who spent his career solving complex energy and engineering challenges, Galm is now investing in the next generation of problem solvers through his support of ARRL.

The gift will directly support ARRL programs and activities, including the ARRL STEM education initiative within the Education and Technology Program, and ARRL’s work defending access to amateur radio spectrum through the Spectrum Defense Fund. He didn’t stop there, however. Dr. Galm expressed how important ARRL is to the future of amateur radio by designating a significant portion of his gift as unrestricted support to the ARRL Diamond Club, which supports ARRL’s general fund, and the Second Century Fund, ARRL’s unrestricted endowment fund, which will be invested and support ARRL operations in perpetuity.

### Green Bank Observatory / National Radio Quiet Zone Reviewing Rules For SOTA/POTA

[AG4VA][ag4va] (a SOTA legend) has been coordinating with the [Green Bank Observatory][gbo] to work on future rules/regulations surrounding [SOTA][sota] operation near the GBO.

One thing that makes the results a little difficult to interpret is that Green Bank doesn't look at ham radio operations quite the same way we normally do. We normally think in terms of transmitter power — 5 watts, 10 watts, 100 watts, etc. Green Bank is concerned with how much RF energy from our transmitters ultimately reaches their radio astronomy equipment. We can't have a conscientious activator follow the published instructions, contact Green Bank before an activation, and then be faced with a technical standard that may be impossible to meet or practically verify on the summit. We need the restrictions to be known before someone plans an activation. More information to follow.

## W3APL Last Month’s Events

- [W3APL][w3apl] held a Re-Inaugural VE testing on campus, mostly focusing at club members and the interns/students that had an interest in ham radio. We are licensed through the [ARRL VEC][vec], and was able to have a great success. We were able to re-test a Ham whose license was lapsed by over 5 years, as well as a club member upgrading their license to General. We had a few that were able to get a good head start for their eventual successful license/upgrades! We plan on having quarterly testing sessions, so look forward to more information in the near future.
- [W3APL][w3apl] club members in conjunction with [Laurel Amateur Radio Club][larc], participated in the [Seven Foot Knoll Lighthouse][lighthouse] activation in support of the [International Lighthouse and Lightship Weekend][illw]. 6 club members setup at the lighthouse and made almost 50 contacts with 7 other lighthouses, not to mention some POTA activators and a state fair. Such a very cool location to operate out of!
- There were numerous participations in [Parks on the Air (POTA)][pota] and digital mode contests (RTTY mostly), including a successful activation of the [Blue Ridge Parkway (US-3378)][us3378], and another club member activated [Patuxent National Wildlife Refuge (US-0334)][us0334], helping show portable HF to a new upgrade!
- A club member participated in [Cat Pictures on the Air (CPOTA)][cpota] on 8/8/26, and was able to successfully receive an SSTV picture of a cat from the Technical University of Hamburg Radio Club [(DL0TUH)][dl0tuh]. All the awesome technology in the world, and we use it to trade cat pictures.
- Another club member activated Mill Mountain [(W4V/HB-023)][w4vhb023], and then completed a hike of 12 miles and a camping trip combined. He is currently well on his way to a Mountain Goat award in SOTA with 696 points! Even typing that whole thing made me tired!
- From an administrative note, W3APL has been testing out a new Discord channel to better communicate with members, and including members without direct access to the lab (moved on, retired, etc).
